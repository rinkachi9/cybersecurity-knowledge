## What it is?

**Shoulder surfing** is a **direct observation attack** where an attacker obtains sensitive information by **watching the victim’s screen, keyboard, or device** without authorization.

It can be:

- **Direct:** Standing nearby and observing in real time.
- **Indirect:** Using tools such as cameras, binoculars, or video recording devices.

### Categorization

- **Vector-Based:** Physical observation (direct or via recording devices).
- **Technique-Based:** Social engineering, physical surveillance.
- **Objective-Based:** Credential theft, personal information collection, surveillance for fraud.

## How it works

1. **Target Selection:**
   - The attacker identifies a potential victim entering sensitive information (e.g., passwords, PINs, security codes).
2. **Positioning:**
   - Physically placing themselves where they can see the victim’s input.
3. **Observation:**
   - Watching keystrokes, screen content, or gestures.
4. **Recording (Optional):**
   - Using smartphones, cameras, or CCTV to capture the data for later use.
5. **Exploitation:**
   - Using stolen data to log in, authorize transactions, or impersonate the victim.

## Common scenarios

- **ATM Machines:** Observing PIN entry.
- **Public Transport:** Watching someone log into online banking on their phone.
- **Airports/Cafés:** Viewing sensitive business documents on a laptop.
- **Workplaces:** Looking over a colleague’s shoulder during login.
- **Conference Events:** Observing unprotected screens during presentations.

## Variants

| **Type** | **Description** | **Example** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct Visual** | Observing directly with the naked eye. | Standing behind a person typing a password. |
| **Optical Aid** | Using binoculars, cameras, or zoom lenses. | Filming from across a room. |
| **Screen Capture Devices** | Hidden cameras placed to record screen activity. | Spycam in meeting room ceiling. |
| **Public Display Eavesdropping** | Viewing large unprotected screens. | Reading confidential slides on a projector. |

## Psychological and human factors

- **Inattentiveness:** Victims unaware of surroundings.
- **Overconfidence:** Belief that “no one is watching.”
- **Crowded Environments:** Assumption of anonymity.
- **Trust Bias:** Ignoring the risk because the observer appears non-threatening.

## Impact

- **Credential Compromise:** Passwords, PINs, security answers stolen.
- **Data Breach:** Business or client information leaked.
- **Financial Loss:** Unauthorized transactions.
- **Privacy Violation:** Exposure of personal or confidential data.

## Detection and prevention

### Detection

- Being alert to unusual positioning of people nearby.
- Spotting unauthorized recording devices.

#### Prevention

- **Technical:**
  - Use **privacy screens** on monitors and laptops.
  - Enable screen auto-lock after short inactivity.
- **Physical:**
  - Shield keyboards or screens with your body or hand.
  - Maintain safe distances when entering sensitive information.
- **Behavioral:**
  - Be aware of surroundings in public spaces.
  - Avoid working on sensitive tasks in high-traffic areas.
- **Organizational:**
  - Implement “Clean Desk” and “Clear Screen” policies.
  - Train employees in physical security awareness.

## Shoulder surfing in modern threat landscape

- Attackers increasingly combine shoulder surfing with **skimming devices** (e.g., at ATMs).
- In hybrid work environments, **remote shoulder surfing** via compromised webcams or cameras in public areas is a growing concern.
- AR glasses and wearable tech make covert observation easier.
